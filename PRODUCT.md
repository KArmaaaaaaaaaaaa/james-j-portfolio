## Platform

web

## Users

**Primary:** the owner of a neighbourhood business — a bar, a garage, a small
shop. Typically 40-65, runs the place themselves, is not technical, and has
either no website or one that has been wrong for two summers. They judge in
seconds and distrust anyone who uses words like "platform" or "solution".

**Situation:** they are losing customers to the business next door because
that one shows up properly on Google. They are not shopping for a website;
they are annoyed that people can't find them.

**Job:** find someone who will do it, say what it costs up front, and not
disappear afterwards.

## What it makes possible

A one-person studio (James J Benavides, brand **James J Projects**) that
delivers four things for a local business:

1. **Google Business Profile** put in order — photos, hours, replies to reviews.
2. **A website**, hand-built, no template.
3. **A menu on the table** — an NFC sticker per table that opens the live menu.
   The sticker carries the table number, so the WhatsApp button arrives
   pre-written with "I'm at table 7". A photocopied code cannot do that.
4. **Photo-to-invoice** — the owner sends a photo of their handwritten day
   sheet and gets a PDF invoice with VAT and withholding calculated.

**The mechanism that is genuinely different:** the client can change their own
prices. The menu, hours, notices and photos live in a panel the owner operates
from their phone; changing a price changes it on every table at once. Everyone
else hands over a brochure that is out of date the day it ships.

## Durable constraints and product truth

**Prices — confirmed and not to be altered:**

| Service | Price |
|---|---|
| Google Business Profile | 150-300 €, one-off |
| Full website | 400-900 €, closed price |
| Maintenance | 30-50 €/month, no lock-in |
| Photo-to-invoice | 250 € setup + 20 €/month |
| Menu on the table (NFC) | 120 € up to 20 tables, +3 € per extra table |

**Written commitments, currently on the site:** the domain is registered in the
client's name; no templates; two commissions at a time; half up front and half
when they like it.

**Honesty markers the site currently carries:** the dashboard demo figures are
invented and labelled as such; the invoice tool explicitly is not tax advice;
photos must be the owner's own, not AI-generated (Google rejects generated
photos on Business Profiles, and EU AI Act Article 50 has required labelling of
authentic-looking generated content since 2 August 2026).

**The only real proof:** an August 2026 invoice for 5,574.00 € reconstructed
from 21 handwritten day entries, matching the hand-issued invoice to the cent.
Everything else on the site is an honest demo, and says so.

**Live artefact:** a working client site at
`sitio-demo-bar.jamesjoelbenavides2004.workers.dev` (Cloudflare Worker, D1, R2)
with a real admin panel. It is linked from the site and can be touched.

## Language

**English**, decided by the owner on 2026-09-07, ahead of a confirmed move to
Hamburg. Flagged at the time: the paying customer today is a Spanish bar owner
who does not read English. The generator is therefore built for two languages
so Spanish can be produced from the same source without a rewrite.

## Constraints

- Static HTML, generated by `_build.py` (English by default, `es` for the
  Spanish build into `/es/`). No framework, no build step, no third-party
  request at all: the three typefaces are self-hosted in `fuentes/` and
  everything else is inline.
- Published on GitHub Pages from `main`.
- Must stay fast on a bad mobile connection: the audience opens it in the
  street. That rules out heavy imagery.
